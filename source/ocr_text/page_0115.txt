My dear Spinlove, 20.8.25.
I think the explanation of your escape is that the impervious excava-
tion holds surface water, which collects against the outside of the walls,
like a tank. This gets under the foundations and through the footings,
and floods the space under the floor-which, I assume, has 4-in. or G-in.
of loose filling-and the water is forced up by its standing head through
the concrete. The probability is that this process began while the concrete
and rendering were green, so that minute channels were then formed in
it ; but under the conditions stated the cellar could scarcely be a dry one, as the floor was not damp-proofed.
If there is such a fall in the ground that you can drain the water
away by connecting a pipe through the wall into the hard core under the
floor, the cure is easy. If not, your only course, in my opinion, is to cover
the floor with asphalt and put a concrete floor on top, to prevent the water
forcing the asphalt up. This concrete, however, may not be necessary.
